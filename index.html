Hey, I'm Rohit.

CS + DS at Berkeley.

I build at the intersection of software, health, and human performance. Right now, I'm working on reducing unwanted side effects from popular GLP medications.

I started as a clueless middle schooler with hackathons and a crusty Java textbook. Somehow, that turned into a run of high school, national, and international hackathon wins, followed by a deep dive into competitive programming. In 2023, I reached USA Computing Olympiad Platinum, placing in the top 1% of competitive programmers, and later helped represent the U.S. at the AGM IX International Programming Contest.

Along the way, I explored research across computational finance, software maintenance, AI, truck routing, and heat-index modeling, working with groups and professors at UChicago, UIUC, Minnesota, and Berkeley. I also coauthored two IEEE papers, led Agentic AI at Swif.ai, and built a daily-use AI dashboard for five AT&T product managers that modeled over $100M in revenue.

But the thread that never really disappeared was biological optimization.

I've always been obsessed with the question: how do you make the human body and mind work better in real life? Not in a vague "wellness" way, but in a practical, systems-level, PubMed-at-2am kind of way. Cognition, recovery, metabolism, health span, performance longevity... I kept coming back to it.

Now I'm combining that obsession with my technical background to build tools that make medicine more personalized, tolerable, and effective.

Still learning. Still experimenting. Still probably reading too many papers at night.
